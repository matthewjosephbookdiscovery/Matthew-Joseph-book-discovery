How to Find Amazon Keywords Readers Actually Search

Most authors choose Amazon keywords the same way: they guess. They think about what their book is about, list a few obvious terms, and move on. The problem is that Amazon keywords are not a description of your book. They are a map of what readers type when they are trying to find a book like yours, and those are rarely the same words an author would choose.

A better starting point is competitive research. Look at the books ranking well in your subcategory and pay attention to the language in their reviews, not just their titles. Readers describe books in their own words, and those words are often more specific and more searched than anything on the cover copy.

Category and keyword strategy work together. A keyword with strong search volume is far less useful if your book is not eligible to rank in the category where that search behavior lives. Before finalizing keywords, confirm the categories you are eligible for and whether they match where your ideal reader is actually browsing.

Finally, treat your keyword list as a living document, not a one-time task. Search behavior shifts with trends, seasons, and new comparable titles entering the market. Revisiting keywords every few months keeps your listing aligned with how readers are searching today, not how they were searching when the book launched.
