The Most Common Mistake in Series Marketing

A recurring pattern shows up when working with authors who write series: each book gets marketed as its own isolated event, disconnected from the ones before it. That approach treats every release as a fresh start, which means the marketing work never compounds.

A stronger approach treats the series itself as the product being marketed, with each new release designed to pull readers backward into the earlier books as much as forward into future ones. That means pricing, promotion timing, and positioning should account for the whole catalog, not just the newest title.

Backlist titles in a series often have more untapped potential than the newest release, because they carry proof in the form of reviews and reader history that a new book has not built yet. A short backlist push timed just before a new release can meaningfully lift the whole series.

The practical shift is simple: before promoting the next book, ask what role it plays in bringing readers into the full series, not just what it does on its own.
